## Collaborative Discussion 1

**Purpose:** To analyse the rise and organisational value of agent-based systems, engage critically with peers and refine the initial argument through feedback.

**My contribution:** I produced the initial analysis, responded to two peers with evidence-based critiques, reviewed feedback on my own post and produced the final synthesis.

**Key development:** The activity moved my analysis from a general discussion of decentralisation and autonomy toward a more precise understanding of emergent behaviour, coordination mechanisms, bounded autonomy, orchestration and human oversight.

**Feedback and action:** Peer feedback identified areas that were underdeveloped in the initial post. These were explicitly addressed in the Unit 3 summary rather than simply acknowledged.

**Learning outcomes:** LO1 and LO4.

## Collaborative Discussion 2

**Purpose:** To compare agent communication languages such as KQML with method invocation in Python and Java, including the advantages and disadvantages of each approach.

**My contribution:** I produced the initial comparison, responded critically to two classmates, challenged specific technical assumptions, proposed practical mitigation measures for semantic and interoperability problems, and submitted the final 300-word synthesis.

**Key development:** The activity moved my analysis beyond a simple contrast between “ACLs for distributed systems” and “methods for conventional programming”. I developed a more precise distinction between communicative intent and procedural interfaces, and recognised that syntactic interoperability is insufficient without shared semantics, ontology governance, validation and conformance.

**Feedback and action:** One direct peer response was received on my initial post. It reinforced the importance of constrained semantics, ontology governance, message validation and permanent logging. I also developed the discussion through my two peer responses, which challenged claims about method invocation and explored practical governance measures.

**Learning outcomes:** LO1 and LO4 for the formal e-portfolio mapping.

## Collaborative Discussion 3

**Purpose:** To evaluate ethical issues associated with generative technologies based on deep learning, including bias, privacy, intellectual property, misinformation, accountability and human oversight.

**My contribution:** I produced the initial analysis, responded critically to two peers, received two substantive peer responses on my own post and used that feedback to refine the final synthesis.

**Key development:** The discussion changed my treatment of human oversight. I moved from presenting human review as a safeguard to recognising that oversight is only meaningful when reviewers have suitable expertise, access to evidence, authority to reject outputs and clearly defined accountability.

**Feedback and action:** Peer feedback strengthened the lifecycle view of AI risk management and challenged the assumption that human involvement automatically improves outcomes. I incorporated continuous evaluation, reporting mechanisms, reviewer capability and distributed accountability into the final summary.

**Learning outcomes:** Supporting evidence for LO3 through consideration of legal, social, ethical and professional issues, and LO4 through structured peer engagement.

## Unit 6 — Agent Dialogue Using KQML and KIF

**Purpose:** To translate a simple procurement scenario into an agent dialogue using KQML as the communication wrapper and KIF as the content language.

**My contribution:** I constructed four messages between Alice and Bob: two `ask-one` queries and two `tell` responses. The dialogue covers both current stock quantity and the number of HDMI slots on the televisions.

**Tools and techniques:** KQML performatives, sender/receiver fields, KIF content expressions and variable binding in a simple request-response pattern.

**Key result:** The artefact demonstrates the difference between the communicative act and the proposition carried by the message. This directly reinforces the conceptual distinction explored in Collaborative Discussion 2.

**Limitations:** The exercise is deliberately simple and does not include ontology declarations, error handling, refusal, negotiation, authentication or executable agent code.

**Feedback:** No specific peer or tutor feedback has been received for this artefact yet.

**Learning outcomes:** Primarily LO1. It also provides conceptual preparation for LO3, but is not treated as full implementation evidence.

**Evidence:** [View completed artefact]({{ '/modules/intelligent-agents/evidence/artefacts/unit-06-agent-dialogue-kqml-kif/' | relative_url }})

## Unit 8 — Constituency-Based Parse Trees

**Purpose:** To represent the phrase structure of three supplied sentences using constituency-based parse trees and identify syntactic ambiguity.

**My contribution:** I constructed the required trees using explicit phrase labels (S, NP, VP and PP) and represented both valid attachments of the prepositional phrase in the ambiguous sentence *The man saw the dog with the telescope*.

**Key result:** The activity demonstrates hierarchical sentence structure and shows how PP attachment can produce different meanings from the same sequence of words.

**Limitations:** This is manual parsing evidence rather than evaluation of an automated parser.

**Learning outcome:** Supporting evidence for LO3 through application of a natural-language processing technique used in intelligent systems.
