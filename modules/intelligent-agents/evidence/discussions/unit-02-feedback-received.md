# Collaborative Discussion 1 — Feedback Received

**Topic:** Agent-Based Systems  
**Unit:** 2  
**Status:** Peer feedback received

## Peer Feedback 1

One peer highlighted three areas for development in my initial post:

- I described unpredictable system-level outcomes but did not explicitly name the concept of **emergent behaviour**.
- I identified coordination costs and conflicting local objectives but did not discuss concrete mitigation mechanisms such as negotiation protocols or contract-net approaches.
- I mentioned human oversight but did not develop this into a specific mechanism, such as adjustable autonomy.

This feedback was useful because it showed that my initial post identified relevant risks but stopped short of fully developing the vocabulary and control mechanisms associated with them.

## Peer Feedback 2

A second peer extended the discussion by focusing on **deterministic orchestration**, structured agent roles and privacy boundaries. Their example used specialist agents for planning, retrieval and evidence assessment while deterministic code controlled execution order and message formats. They also argued that a central policy boundary can constrain access to sensitive information without necessarily removing all agent autonomy.

The key question raised was whether a deterministic orchestrator makes a system less genuinely agent-based or whether agents can remain autonomous within bounded roles and permissions.

## Action taken

I incorporated these ideas into the Unit 3 summary by:

- naming **emergent behaviour** explicitly;
- discussing coordination mechanisms and communication semantics;
- developing the idea of **bounded autonomy**;
- arguing that deterministic orchestration can coexist with agent autonomy when agents retain meaningful local decision-making within defined roles;
- extending human oversight into approval points, escalation rules and adjustable autonomy.

This changed my framing from a simple centralised-versus-decentralised comparison to a broader design problem concerning the distribution of autonomy, coordination and oversight.
