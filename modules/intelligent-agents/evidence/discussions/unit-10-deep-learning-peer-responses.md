# Collaborative Discussion 3 — Peer Responses

**Topic:** Deep Learning  
**Unit:** 10  
**Status:** Submitted

The text below reproduces my two submitted peer responses.

## Peer Response 1

Yasmin, I agree that hallucination is particularly dangerous in academic systems because generated claims and references can appear credible even when they are incorrect. Walters and Wilder (2023) demonstrate this clearly through fabricated and erroneous bibliographic citations. Your use of retrieval, verification and an execution trace in ScholarPlan therefore seems like a sensible way of reducing blind reliance on generated output.

However, I would argue that verification mechanisms should themselves be treated as fallible. If one model generates a claim and another automated component checks it, both may still rely on incomplete retrieval, incorrect metadata or similar underlying assumptions. Preventive measures could therefore include checking claims against primary sources, requiring source identifiers such as DOI values where possible, recording confidence or verification status, and escalating uncertain claims for human review rather than presenting them as verified.

On your question about responsibility, I do not think it should fall entirely on either the developer or the user. Responsibility should depend partly on control and foreseeable risk. Developers are responsible for designing reasonable safeguards, communicating limitations and avoiding interfaces that create unjustified confidence. Users also have responsibilities when they rely on outputs in contexts where verification is expected. UNESCO (2021) and NIST (2024) both emphasise accountability and human oversight rather than assuming that responsibility disappears once an AI system is deployed.

For an academic research agent, I would therefore favour shared but clearly defined responsibility: the system should provide traceable evidence, while the researcher remains responsible for validating claims before relying on them.

Would you allow ScholarPlan to mark a source as “verified” automatically, or would final verification always require human confirmation?

## Peer Response 2

Frederick, I agree that copyright, academic integrity and misinformation are among the most significant ethical problems associated with generative AI. I particularly like your suggestion that creators could explicitly approve whether their material is used for training. However, I think consent alone may be difficult to implement because training datasets can contain material collected from many sources, while ownership and licensing conditions may not always be obvious.

A stronger approach may therefore combine consent mechanisms with greater dataset transparency, licensing information and technical controls. For academic use, I also agree with Kovari (2025) that clearer institutional policies are necessary. However, preventing misuse probably requires changes to assessment design as well. Tasks that require students to explain decisions, defend sources or demonstrate stages of their work may be more resistant to undisclosed AI-generated submissions than simply banning particular tools.

Your question about labelling synthetic content is especially important. A universal visible watermark may not be sufficient because labels can be removed through editing, screenshots or re-encoding. A more robust solution could combine visible labelling with cryptographically verifiable provenance metadata showing how and when content was created or modified. Even then, provenance systems would only work if platforms and software vendors adopt compatible standards.

I therefore think the goal should not be to make synthetic content impossible to misuse, which is unrealistic, but to increase traceability and make deception harder. NIST (2024) similarly emphasises provenance, transparency and risk-management controls when addressing generative-AI risks.

Do you think responsibility for preserving provenance should fall mainly on AI developers, social-media platforms, or the person publishing the content?
