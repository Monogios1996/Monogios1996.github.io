# Collaborative Discussion 3 — Summary Post

**Topic:** Deep Learning  
**Unit:** 11  
**Status:** Submitted

The text below reproduces my submitted summary.

## Summary Post

My initial post argued that generative AI offers benefits but raises ethical concerns involving bias, privacy, intellectual property and misinformation. I viewed these as sociotechnical problems requiring safeguards throughout development and deployment. The peer feedback strengthened this position while exposing a weakness in my proposed solution: human review cannot simply be assumed to provide effective protection.

Etuk (2026) emphasised that safeguards should operate throughout the AI lifecycle. His suggestions concerning dataset provenance, consent, sensitive-data filtering and monitoring helped me connect preventive measures with continuing responsibility after deployment. This aligns with NIST (2024), which addresses generative AI risks across design, development, use and evaluation. I would therefore extend my original recommendations to include repeated testing and accessible mechanisms for reporting harmful or reproduced content.

Koller (2026) challenged my reliance on human oversight. Sele and Chugunova (2024) found that allowing participants to adjust recommendations increased uptake of automated decision support but reduced decision accuracy in their experiment. Although this was a specific prediction task rather than generative AI, it demonstrates why human involvement should not automatically be equated with better outcomes. I now consider reviewer expertise, access to evidence and authority to reject outputs essential conditions whose effectiveness must also be evaluated.

Her accountability argument also clarified that responsibility requires more than identifying a final reviewer. Novelli, Taddeo and Floridi (2024) describe accountability through answerability between an agent and a forum. In practice, I would require documented responsibilities and escalation routes across developers, deploying organisations and reviewers. I consider meaningful authority difficult to guarantee when organisational pressures favour rapid approval.

My main learning is that evaluating an intelligent system involves assessing both its outputs and the arrangements governing its use. I still support responsible adoption, but would judge safeguards by demonstrated effectiveness, including whether people can detect, challenge and correct harmful outcomes.
